      Detective profile

      The geologist as detective

      Nobody watched these rocks being deposited, folded, faulted or buried. There is no eyewitness, only what was left behind, so every interpretation reconstructs past events from remains, as a detective reconstructs a crime from a scene.

      Each case starts where real interpretation starts: a seismic line, well logs, sometimes core. The work is to build the best-supported case the evidence allows and to state how much confidence that case deserves.

      Some cases close cleanly. Many close with more than one explanation still standing, because that is the usual state of subsurface evidence.

      Why more than one answer survives

      The evidence is non-unique

      Different subsurface configurations can produce the same, or nearly the same, seismic response. Working back from recorded data to the rocks is therefore [[non-uniqueness|non-unique]], and more computation does not remove that. Explanations can be ranked by plausibility, with the remaining uncertainty stated.

      A well is the closest thing to ground truth, and it carries uncertainty of its own: [[calibration|tool calibration]], [[cuttings-lag|lag of cuttings]] on the way to surface, and whether the [[depth-uncertainty|depth logged]] matches the depth the rock came from.

      Every case therefore shows several suspects, and the case report compares the confidence placed in each with a panel of interpreters. The measure is how closely confidence tracks the strength of the evidence, not how many cases are solved.

      How to play

      Working a case

      1. Evidence. Clues are grouped in lines of evidence: seismic, well logs, attributes and machine learning. Next clue (or the arrow keys) steps through them in order; any note can also be opened directly. Examine the sticky notes. Each one is circled on the seismic line or well logs, and can be strung to the suspects it supports or argues against. Tops can be picked and correlated on the logs.
      2. Suspects. Share 100% of your confidence among the suspects with the sliders. The uncertainty timeline records how the spread of confidence changes after each note.
      3. Leads. A limited number of further data requests, such as core or a 3D survey, can be made from the chalkboard.
      4. Close the case. The case report compares your distribution with the panel after the same notes, shows how much each note and lead narrows the field, and states what is known and with what confidence.
