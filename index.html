    How to play

    Each case is a piece of subsurface with more than one possible explanation. The task is to decide how much confidence each explanation deserves, based on the seismic and the wells.

    1. Examine the seismic. Look at how reflections are offset, thicken, thin, bend or end. Depth and two-way time can both be displayed.
    2. Pick tops in the wells. Switch between log types, pick the same surface in each well, and flatten on a pick to compare thicknesses. Logs carry real-world problems such as washouts, old tools and depth offsets.
    3. Weigh the interpretations. Share 100% of your confidence among the candidates with the sliders, and tick the evidence your distribution rests on.
    4. Submit and compare. The debrief shows your distribution next to a panel of interpreters, what is actually known about the case and how confident that knowledge is, and the panel's picks. An optional evidence check lists evidence the panel rated as diagnostic that was not ticked.

    There is no single right answer to hit. The score is the overlap between your distribution and the panel's, where 100% means identical.

    Underlined words open a short definition. Easy cases have clean data; hard and advanced cases have poorer data, fewer wells, or none.
